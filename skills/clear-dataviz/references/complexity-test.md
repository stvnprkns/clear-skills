# Complexity Test

## Principle

A specialized form must beat a strong standard baseline on the primary task by enough to justify its learning, implementation, accessibility, and verification costs.

## Use when

- topology and quantity must be perceived together;
- high-density periodic or multiscale patterns exceed a normal line chart;
- multivariate constraint or composition is the question;
- ensemble behavior or uncertainty shape changes the decision;
- spatial density is more useful than individual points.

## Reject when

- the baseline already answers the question;
- the form primarily creates novelty or brand distinction;
- readers need exact lookup the form cannot support;
- data transformations are unavailable or unverifiable;
- the form fails in required media with no adequate fallback.

## Prefer instead

Write a comparison:

| Candidate | Primary task success | Information lost | Learning cost | Failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard baseline | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Specialized form | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Choose the specialized form only when its gain is decision-relevant, not merely visible.

## Escape conditions

- An expert audience can justify higher learning cost.
- A public explainer can teach one unfamiliar form if the teaching cost buys a major insight.
- Exploratory tools may offer several linked forms when each answers a distinct question.

## Audit signals

- no stated baseline;
- form name substitutes for task reasoning;
- explanation spends more effort teaching the chart than the data;
- static or mobile fallback changes the claim;
- interaction is required for basic orientation.
