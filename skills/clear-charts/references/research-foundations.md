# Research Foundations

Use research to constrain judgment, not to manufacture universal laws. Each chain ends in observable review behavior.

## Graphical perception

### Finding

Controlled studies generally find higher accuracy for position on a common scale than for length, angle, area, volume, or color judgments; task and implementation still affect performance.

### Clear principle

Put a precision-critical comparison on a common position or aligned length when the data and task allow it.

### Escape conditions

Area, angle, and color remain useful for approximate part-to-whole, density, spatial pattern, and overview. Familiarity and compactness may outweigh small precision gains when exact comparison is not required.

### Review behavior

Do not automatically replace every bubble or pie chart. Ask what accuracy the decision requires and whether the alternative preserves the intended structure.

Primary basis: Cleveland & McGill (1984); Heer & Bostock (2010).

## Visual aggregation and ensemble perception

### Finding

Viewers can perceive aggregate properties of groups, while crowded displays still impair identification and precise comparison of individual items.

### Clear principle

Use overview encodings for distribution, density, or typicality; use labels, selection, faceting, or a secondary view for individual identity.

### Escape conditions

Do not facet automatically when the question is an ensemble pattern or same-position comparison. Do not summarize when every item is actionable.

### Review behavior

For many series or categories, identify whether the task concerns the set, an individual, or both before reducing marks.

Primary basis: Szafir, Haroz, Gleicher & Franconeri (2016).

## Color difference

### Finding

Equal numeric steps in a color specification do not guarantee equal perceived differences; mark size and spatial context affect discriminability.

### Clear principle

Evaluate categorical and ordered palettes in the rendered mark size and background, not as isolated swatches.

### Escape conditions

Do not reject a palette by category count alone when stable identity across coordinated views is valuable and labels provide redundancy.

### Review behavior

Report observed collisions or weak contrast. If only source is available, mark rendered discriminability unverified.

Primary basis: Szafir (2018).

## Uncertainty and decisions

### Finding

The uncertainty view that supports best effect-size estimation may not support best decision-making; emphasizing means can lead viewers to discount uncertainty.

### Clear principle

Choose uncertainty encodings against the decision and likely reasoning strategy, not by statistical completeness alone.

### Escape conditions

Do not add elaborate distributions when uncertainty cannot change the action or the data does not support them.

### Review behavior

Ask whether plausible outcomes cross a threshold, reorder options, or change confidence. Avoid unsupported significance claims from interval overlap.

Primary basis: Hullman et al. (2019); Kale, Kay & Hullman (2021).

## Change, animation, and memory

### Finding

People can miss substantial changes across visual disruptions, and serial comparison imposes memory costs.

### Clear principle

Keep comparison anchors persistent and use transitions to preserve object identity. Prefer simultaneous comparison when exact before/after judgment matters.

### Escape conditions

Animation can explain process or causal sequence when paced, controllable, and paired with persistent state.

### Review behavior

Reject auto-advancing views that require remembering prior values. Preserve animation that genuinely explains change and supports pause/replay or reduced motion.

Primary basis: Rensink, O'Regan & Clark (1997).

## Visualization literacy

### Finding

Readers vary in their ability to decode visualizations; literacy is measurable rather than safely assumed.

### Clear principle

Match unfamiliar encodings, transformations, and interaction to the audience and consequence of error.

### Escape conditions

Do not flatten expert analytical tools into beginner graphics when trained users gain meaningful capability from the richer form.

### Review behavior

Name the assumed audience. Explain unfamiliar scales or provide a lower-cost entry point when misreading would matter.

Primary basis: Boy et al. (2014).
